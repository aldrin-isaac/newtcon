### Three rubber-stampish cross-checks (where to look)

These are less alarming than they look — three is the operator's
framing, not the auditor's threshold. The samples below represent
the lowest-friction approvals; none of the three show outright
rubber-stamping, but each shows the cross-check seat producing a
short, mechanical-shaped approval without exercising adversarial
attention. The pattern is acceptable on small, well-scoped PRs and
risky on large ones.

1. **PR #70 (Critic approval shape).** "CRITIC-APPROVED — Contract
   PR. 7 binding checks: 1. newtron consumption rule — PASS. Only
   API_CONTRACT.md edited..." The 7-check format is followed but
   each check resolves to one sentence; the PR is a 200-line
   contract adaptation to landed newtron#21 substrate. The Critic
   does not surface any consistency risk on a PR that introduces a
   new typed envelope shape — possible because the shape is
   genuinely faithful to the landed substrate, but also possible
   because the check format has settled into a template. Cross-
   compare with N6/PR #78 where the same Critic format catches a
   real failure: the format is honest, but on routine PRs it
   short-circuits to checklist completion.

2. **PR #67 (Architecture Reviewer approval shape).** "ARCHREV-
   APPROVED — Faithful adaptation to landed bare-substrate shapes;
   binding substrate-vs-decoration boundary makes §46 load-bearing
   at the contract layer." The body's adversarial check list is
   present but each item is one-line. For a 153-addition / 67-
   deletion PR with cross-section dependencies, more substantive
   adversarial probing of "what alternative shape did the Architect
   not consider?" would honor the seat's purpose better. Not a
   rejection-worthy failure on this PR but a pattern to watch on
   the next gap-adaptation cycle.

3. **The closed PR #43 (Composer hardening, 5 schemas bundled).**
   The Architect bundled five issues into one Contract PR.
   No reviewer caught the bundling as a process risk; PR was
   closed and the work redistributed into PR #54 + smaller siblings
   only after the operator-level review. The 5-in-1 bundle was
   appropriate by the Architect's own reasoning (shared substrate)
   but the *meta-process* concern — "a Contract PR bundling 5
   issues is harder to review adversarially than 5 PRs of 1 issue
   each" — surfaced only in the operator's review. Both the Critic
   and Architecture Reviewer should add an implicit check: "is the
   PR's scope shape going to obscure the adversarial review?" That
   meta-check is not in the role specs today; consider adding it.

### Why mission divergence was not detected

The Critic's 7 binding checks and Architecture Reviewer's 9
adversarial checks evaluate **PR shape**, not **cumulative
trajectory**. The Tech Lead is spawned per-feature and was not
spawned because nobody dispatched a feature into the lead's queue.
The Drift Auditor is the cumulative-trajectory seat and was dormant
until operator invocation. Three structural fixes prevent
recurrence:

1. The charter amendment (A1) makes "ship the in-flight surface
   before re-saturating the contract backlog" binding rather than
   advisory.
2. Periodic Drift Auditor cadence — the role spec says "weekly cron"
   but no cron is wired. Until it is, the operator must invoke;
   beyond that, every Friday-of-week is the minimum. Otherwise the
   cumulative-trajectory check exists only when the operator
   notices.
3. The Tech Lead's invocation trigger should be widened from "on
   feature kickoff" to "on feature kickoff OR when 3+ consecutive
   merges advance no operator surface." That widening turns "we're
   doing exhaustive contract work" into a structural trigger,
   not an operator-judgment trigger.
